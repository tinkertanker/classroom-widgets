=== GENERAL NOTES ===
[mac] top3=notch4,mascot4,star3
  All five now share one base (faceted amber plate, cream die-cut, rainbow ring) and differ only in what sits on it. That proves the ring is the real brand asset, but it leaves the final with little range. Every second object (hamster, star, card) buys recognition in the colour icon and costs legibility in the 16–18 px glyph. The only tray mark that is both distinctive and clean is the bare notched ring, and even that is about 2x SF weight with an uneven counter. Nothing carries the name-picker cue down to small sizes, star3 and board3 are byte-identical to round 3, and no concept is built for macOS 26 Icon Composer yet: baked sticker shadows and plate facets should be separate layers so Liquid Glass supplies the depth, and the tinted/clear renders need checking in Icon Composer rather than just being claimed. Push a responsive system rather than three rival marks: notch4's ring as the constant (tray glyph with the live countdown, 16–32 px master), mascot4's hamster-in-the-face as the hero from 64 px up, and star3 as the conservative alternative.

[winlinux] top3=star3,notch4,hamnotch4
  Four of the five are the same warm rainbow ring on the same amber plate, told apart only by an add-on, and at my sizes the add-on is what carries identity. The bare ring (notch4) reads as a ring-chart utility such as Disk Usage Analyzer, Filelight or DaisyDisk; the hamsters only survive in colour at 32px and up; and the star is the only add-on that survives as a 16px white tray glyph. None of the glyphs is hand-hinted to the pixel grid (grey fringes at 18/20px, notch4's lumpy counter, board3's half-pixel card edge), tray weight runs from Fluent-light (star3) to a solid coin (mascot4), and the mock bars have no real Wi-Fi, volume or battery neighbours, so weight matching was never actually tested. For the hand-off, push a star3 × notch4 merge (star3's star and geometry with notch4's warm sweep and visible depletion notch), then build pixel-hinted glyph masters at 16/20/22/24/32px (GNOME symbolic SVG, KDE Breeze 22px with ColorScheme-Text, Windows at 100/125/150%) and a full .ico ladder that includes 20/24/40/48px. Still missing: a tray-worthy form of the name picker, and a plate-less die-cut variant, which would feel more native in Windows 11 Start and a GNOME grid than a macOS-style plate stretched to full-bleed.

[tray] top3=notch4,star3,hamnotch4
  Every concept finally replaces the dot grid, and all five glyphs are built on the same rainbow ring, so the deciding question is which add-on survives at 16–18px: the notch does, the star does down to 18px, and the hamster and the name card do not. Nothing is hand-hinted: each glyph is the 1024-unit vector scaled down, leaving 25–50% grey pixels at 18px, and the 'thin spent track' device fails at tray size in both notch4 (lopsided counter, stepped-spiral read) and star3 (only 0.6px thinner, so invisible). Design for 16px at 1x, because that is what school-issued Windows laptops at 100% scaling will show; the retina 36px, where the hamsters almost survive, is the flattering case. The strongest final system is one tray glyph for every option: notch4's ring, hinted, with a true-circle counter and a 1px spent hairline, paired with whichever colour icon the client prefers (bare ring, hamster in the bite, or star). Still missing before handoff: pixel-snapped 16/18/32/36 masters, the promised live-countdown tray state actually rendered at 1x, and a check beside the real Spotlight, Control Centre and wifi glyphs.

[brand] top3=mascot4,star3,notch4
  All five follow the no-numeral rule, and both hamsters are faithful to creatures.tsx. But four of the five are the same amber squircle around a cream-faced rainbow ring, so the set is coherent but narrow. The ring alone isn't ownable: it is the default timer shape (Classroomscreen ships the same timer and name picker), and rainbow-gradient loops now signal AI (Microsoft 365 Copilot's ribbon, Apple Intelligence's glow). The one asset no competitor can claim is the app's own hamster, and the set shows the trade-off plainly: the more a concept leans on it, the worse its glyph. mascot4's knockout smears and hamnotch4's lump turns the ring into a refresh arrow, while the hamster-free glyphs (star3, notch4) are the crispest. The system to ship is a split: mascot4's colour icon as the face of the brand, paired with a clean notched-ring outline glyph at star3's weight, with the hamster carrying docs, onboarding and marketing. Before sign-off: add a wordmark lock-up, run a large one-colour/print test (notch4's lumpy glyph centre shows why), and keep every hamster reduction in side profile, because a round head with two round ears in silhouette is Mickey Mouse's three circles.

[risk] top3=hamnotch4,notch4,star3
  All five are now the same rainbow progress ring on the same amber plate, so the main risks are shared. In 2026 a smooth rainbow ring reads as the Apple Intelligence/Copilot AI gradient or as a colour-picker app, and it will date fastest where the ring is the whole mark (notch4); the widget's real horizontal gradient (star3) is truer to the app and less tied to the trend. The look-alike risk sits in the tray glyphs: at 16–18px every ring with a bump or attachment turns into a system symbol (↺ undo/Time Machine, refresh, Spotlight's magnifier, calendar-with-clock), so settle on one clean notched-ring glyph with a clearly thin track and test it next to Spotlight and Time Machine in a real menu bar. Before any trademark filing, redraw the Font Awesome star used in star3 and board3; the hamster is hand-built art in creatures.tsx and can be owned. Other cultural misreads are low (no crosses, hazard or religious symbols, and the rainbow isn't striped, so it doesn't read as a Pride flag), but keep 'hamster wheel' out of the marketing copy.
